# ambition

## What it does

Before any work, the agent writes a four-part **ambition plan**: the goal behind the goal (**altitude**), the best practitioner's raw material and instruments (**depth**), two or three other fields and what they would do (**range**), and the **outside judge** that will say whether the result is right. Then it does the work with the bar set high, and closes with an honest report of what it reached and verified.

## When to reach for it

Type `/ambition` before a task that matters: a review, an analysis, a feature, a piece of writing you will put your name on. Skip it for quick lookups and one-line fixes.

## Common questions

**Does it ask me a lot of questions?** No. It looks up facts itself and only asks you when altitude changes *what* gets delivered (for example, a decision memo instead of a deck).

**Why does it insist on a judge?** Raising the bar without a judge raises the pressure to look successful. The judge keeps the extra ambition honest.

**Can I run just one direction?** Yes. Type `/altitude`, `/depth`, `/range` or `/outside-judge` directly.

## It's working if

- The plan names concrete things: a specific practitioner, an instrument, a field, a check.
- The final report separates verified, unverified and failed claims.
- At least once, the agent proposes a better road than the one you asked for.
