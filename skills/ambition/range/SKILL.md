# Range

**Range** brings in more fields than a task needs on the surface. The heist-movie move: assemble a crew whose skills nobody would combine by default.

## 1. Turn the noun into verbs

Rewrite the task's noun as what it does. "A quarterly review deck" becomes "get busy people to believe a story about cause and effect, and decide under uncertainty, in 30 minutes." Name who must believe, decide or do what, in how long, under what uncertainty.

## 2. Assemble the crew

List 4 to 6 fields whose professionals are best at those verbs. Include at least one surprising field, and one **adversary** whose job is to bring the work down (a short seller, a forensic accountant, a competitor, a penetration tester).

## 3. Do what each one does

For each crew member, write their method, their check and their output, then carry it out. **Do, not write like**: what a short seller does is research the position against you, so do that research. Imitating their voice adds nothing.

When parallel agents are available, give each crew member one job with its own output, and merge the outputs yourself.

## 4. Try one foreign instrument

Pick the most promising tool or medium from outside the field (3D rendering, simulation, sound design, experimental design, maps, a game engine) and build a small working experiment with it now. A small built experiment shows more than a list of ideas.

## 5. Merge and judge

Report what each crew member changed in the work and what stays open. Then call the Skill tool with "outside-judge" before calling the work done.

Done when every crew member left a concrete change or artifact, and the adversary's strongest attack is either answered or listed as open.
